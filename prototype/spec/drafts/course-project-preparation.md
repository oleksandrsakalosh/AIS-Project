The course application can stay a local modular monolith using Python, FastAPI, Jinja2, SQLite and the existing small browser layer. Views 01 and 11 guide the client and staff portals, scheduling, reservations, memberships, payments and notifications. Views 03 and 17 also identify the authentication/permissions responsibility. Implement these as logical owners inside one application and SQLite file. Reuse the existing transactional booking service and UTC/Europe/Bratislava handling. Use fictional data and a loopback server, without public hosting, live payments, external messaging or a native mobile app. GAP CP-06 spreadsheet migration and the historical reception-cash transition are outside the proposed local delivery. Upgrading an existing **local SQLite** database in place is a compatibility need and should be verified on disposable copies.

## Implementation increments

| **INC-01 · Client registration and own profile** (R-01, access part of R-07, CP-01) | v0.1.1 baseline; DEC-C08 | A visitor registers in Slovak, signs in through existing login and can update their own display name. Duplicate signup and altered identity/role fields are rejected. The account and profile persist. The new client has no effective membership and receives the existing booking refusal. Seeded clients and trainer still pass booking/API regressions. |
| **INC-02 · Account and role administration** (R-07, CP-01) | INC-01; DEC-C01, DEC-C09 | A local bootstrap administrator manages supported account states and provisions staff. | **INC-03 · Trainers, activities and rooms** (R-05, CP-02) | INC-02; DEC-C02 | Administrator can add, edit and deactivate each catalog type and explicitly link a trainer login to its record. Non-admin writes fail. |
| **INC-04 · Trainer scheduling and capacity** (R-04, R-02 integration, CP-03/05) | INC-03; DEC-C02–04 | Linked trainers can add and edit their own future sessions under the approved permissions. Another trainer's write and trainer/room overlaps fail. Clients see approved edits in the same schedule. A capacity reduction racing a booking cannot leave confirmed occupancy above capacity. |
| **INC-05 · Membership selection and history** (R-03, CP-04) | INC-01, DEC-C05/06 | Client can select a seeded plan, see their price/duration, save a pending checkout, reopen it and see their own membership/history. | **INC-06 · Simulated purchase and renewal** (R-03 adaptation of CP-07) | INC-05; DEC-C05/06 | A visibly simulated checkout exercises success, decline and timeout. Only success grants one entitlement; replay and concurrent success cannot double-grant. Renewal respects period boundaries and I-02. Client can inspect their persisted outcome and history; audit data records who, what and when. |
| **INC-07 · In-app inbox** (R-06 adaptation of CP-08; local AC-02-1 coverage) | INC-03/04, existing R-02, DEC-C07 | A booking, first cancellation and a relevant training update produce the specified recipients' notices. An unrelated client sees none. Unread/read state survives reload and restart, repeats produce no duplicate notices, and inbox failure leaves a committed reservation intact. |

## Decisions for the user

DEC-C08 is confirmed in the [INC-01 decision record](../inc-01-client-account.md). The other recommendations are open. The listed increment identifies when each answer affects a contract.

| Decision | What the sources leave open and the recommended answer | Consequences |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DEC-C01 · Reception permissions (INC-02) | Views 11, 16 and 17 show reception in internal/reservation workflows, while R-02 and the GAP transition emphasize client self-service. Start reception with authenticated, read-only schedule and aggregate occupancy. Restrict client details, proxy booking, membership, payments and account changes to their separately authorized workflows. | Reception can answer availability questions. Staff-assisted booking or cash handling would require a new permission and contract, including how to authorize action for a client. |
| DEC-C02 · Trainer ownership (INC-03/04) | R-04 names the trainer. Views and code supply no login-to-trainer ownership rule. Link at most one trainer login to one trainer record by explicit administrator action. Let trainers write their own future sessions only, deriving trainer identity from the link. Keep unlinked records readable and deny writes for unlinked logins. Admin manages the link and catalog, with future confirmed assignments protecting reassignment. | The two existing trainer records and one trainer login can be migrated without guessing identity. Client schedule and staff aggregate reads still work. Substitution and cross-trainer edits need a separate policy. |
| DEC-C03 · Scheduling conflicts (INC-04) | C-60 sets booking capacity, while scheduling collision rules are unspecified. Require a future start, end after start and positive capacity. Reject overlapping intervals for the same trainer **or** room, allow adjacent intervals, and check within the write transaction. Keep UTC instants and validate ambiguous or invalid Bratislava inputs explicitly. | Concurrent saves cannot create double allocation. Different rooms and trainers can run in parallel. Capacity is stored per training; physical room limits can be specified separately if needed. Check existing data before enforcing writes. |
| DEC-C04 · Changes to booked trainings (INC-04) | The source provides change notices but gives no rules for rescheduling, deletion or capacity reductions. Freeze time, trainer, room and activity on a training once it has a reservation record, including cancelled history. For future trainings allow capacity edits at or above current confirmed occupancy. Block changes to started sessions and defer staff cancellation/rescheduling of booked sessions. | No silent history rewrite, displaced customer or weakened final-seat invariant. Bookings and capacity saves share the write transaction. Notifications cover allowed changes. Staff needing an exception would need an explicit policy and client consequences. |
| DEC-C05 · Membership selection and renewal (INC-05/06) | I-02 permits at most one active membership. C-02's example rejects an already active membership and gives no renewal policy. Allow one future prepaid renewal. Start an initial/late purchase at successful settlement and schedule an early renewal at the current period end. Store each paid period separately with inclusive start/exclusive end and server-derived terms. Reject another renewal while one is scheduled. | One effective entitlement at any instant and a visible history. The current-membership read and booking check use the effective active period even when there is a future renewal. The proposal refines C-02 for early renewal and requires date-boundary and concurrent checkout tests. |
| DEC-C06 · Simulated payment consistency (INC-05/06) | C-03 gives decline and gateway timeout examples; I-03 and ADR-003 concern an external system. Use an explicitly labelled local scenario control, with server-set plan amount/currency and no real payment details. Persist each attempt as successful, declined or timed out. Only a successful attempt records a simulated settlement and creates its entitlement in the same SQLite transaction. Repeating the same attempt identity returns the recorded result; a new explicit attempt may follow a decline or terminal simulated timeout. After transport uncertainty, first read the persisted attempt status. | Failed attempts appear as such and grant zero entitlement. A duplicate or concurrent request cannot double-settle a checkout, and commit failure rolls back settlement and entitlement together. Audit retains who/what/when and separates attempted outcomes from a confirmed payment. This verifies a local analogue of I-03. |
| DEC-C07 · Notification recipients and reliability (INC-07) | R-06 identifies client and trainer, while event membership, persistence and cancellation are unspecified. Confirmed booking and first client cancellation notify the owner and the trainer account assigned at commit. A meaningful change to a future training notifies its assigned trainer, including when acting as editor, and clients with confirmed reservations at commit. Skip the trainer recipient if there is no linked account. Keep unrelated clients and staff out. Store a durable event and recipient references in the same local transaction, keyed by the actual change, then display from the local inbox with per-recipient read state. | Replays/no-ops and rejected writes create no extra alerts; historical seeding generates none. Inbox read or display failure cannot undo a booking. A core SQLite write failure is still a rolled-back operation. Notices disclose only the training and change information each recipient needs. |
| DEC-C08 · Signup and profile policy (INC-01) | R-01 leaves identity fields, validation and login flow open. Project PDF p. 18 describes a waiting membership after registration. Adopt the [first increment's proposal](increment-01-client-registration.md): username/password/display name, explicit login, server-assigned client role, no membership row until a plan results in entitlement. Retain the source lifecycle as a documented divergence. | The account can use the authenticated browser without gaining booking eligibility. No fabricated dates, plan or payment are needed. This decision is needed for the first contract. |
| DEC-C09 · Account and role lifecycle (INC-02) | R-07 gives the administrator responsibility but no lifecycle. Use one role per account and preserve client/staff account types. Allow admin to provision staff, switch supported staff roles only when trainer assignments permit, and disable/re-enable accounts. Revoke affected sessions on disabling or role change, protect the last active administrator, and reject disabling a client with a future confirmed booking until it is resolved. Preserve identities and history. | Role changes have a clear authorization boundary. Check deactivation and booking consistently under concurrent writes so deactivation cannot lock out the owner of a future seat. A business override or hard deletion would require separate rules and evidence. |
